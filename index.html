Safety and evidence

Comprehensive does not mean uncritical.

Traditional, complementary, clinical, and public-health knowledge can share one home while still being labeled accurately and assessed honestly.

01 · SOURCE

Who is responsible?

Look for authorship, qualifications, institutional responsibility, funding, editorial review, references, and a visible update date.

02 · EVIDENCE

What supports the claim?

Separate historical use and personal testimony from observational research, controlled trials, systematic reviews, and professional guidance.

03 · CONTEXT

Who is the information for?

Age, pregnancy, allergies, diagnoses, medicines, supplements, disability, culture, access, and personal goals can change what is safe.

04 · INTERACTIONS

Everything can interact.

Medicines, herbs, supplements, foods, procedures, and health conditions may affect one another. A pharmacist is an important safety resource.

05 · CHILD SAFETY

Children are not small adults.

Age, weight, concentration, formulation, development, allergies, and supervision matter. Confirm uncertain pediatric dosing professionally.

06 · PRIVACY

Keep health records private.

Do not place personal health information, medicine lists, student records, or appointment notes in public repositories or untrusted forms.
